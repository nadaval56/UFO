PREFACE 
Importance of the Work 
There has been over six decades of intensive activity of advanced 
aerospace craft in the skies over North America and around the world. These have 
been witnessed, photographed and chased by military pilots, witnessed by ground 
crews, by commercial airline pilots and by thousands of military and civilian 
ground based personnel. The importance of this study lies in the possibility that 
some of these advanced aerospace craft may represent a threat to the security of 
the United States including its airspace and nuclear facilities. It is thus a very high 
priority to further our understanding of the physics and engineering feasibility of 
advanced aerospace weapon systems applications as they apply to the potential 
future capabilities of foreign threat systems out to the far term, i.e. from now out 
through the year 2050. 
Appreciation 
BAASS appreciates the high level of trust that has been bestowed on the 
organization by the sponsors/DIA in assigning us with the responsibility of 
furthering our understanding of the physics and engineering feasibility of 
advanced aerospace weapons system applications. BAASS understands the 
sobering importance and the long term implications of this project.

PROJECT DATABASE 
This program will address all twelve technical areas. 
Objectives 
For six decades there have been displays of advanced aerospace vehicles in 
the skies of North America and around the world that have been witnessed by 
thousands of people, including military pilots and ground crews, civil aviation 
pilots, law enforcement officers and members of the public. Because these 
displays have not been taken seriously, few significant resources have been 
allocated to gathering the data into a single place from all of these sightings. 
Instead, data is scattered through over twenty smaller databases in the United 
States and over a dozen more internationally. Because of a lack of resources (a) 
these data have never been brought into a single storage site and (b) 
consequently have never been analyzed using sophisticated search parameters. 
This BAASS project seeks to eliminate this shortcoming. The objective is to create 
the world's first comprehensive high quality searchable database of advanced 
aerial technology maneuvers.

encounter datasets already merged and searchable by keywords from a subset of 
publicly available databases. It is anticipated that the end result of the worldwide 
database collection will results in thousands of different high quality aerospace

II. 
RELIABUITY OF SOURCE: 
This report was made on 6 October 1953 by, Raleigh Iyapanna, an Eskimo of 
Little Diomede Island. Every night during the week preceding the report he had 
maintained a watch on Big Diomede on his own initiative. 
ROY N. FOWLER states 
he believes the source to be fairly reliable and that the sighting of the light 
is possibly true. 
Source is in good health and has excellent eyesight. 
III. 
REPORT OF SIGHTING: 
At approximately 0810252, the source observed a light on the southern 
tip of Big Diomede Island at an estimated height of 95 feet above sea level. 
He 
had been standing in the center of Little Diomede Village looking in the general 
direction of Big Diomede for about fifteen minutes prior to the sighting. Source 
observed the light when it appeared and watched it until it disappeared. The 
duration of the observation was estimated as lasting for several seconds. The 
light was described as being the size of a nickel coin held at arms length and about 
the same color as a burning match or dim flashlight. It was the sources opinion 
that the light came f~om a large flashlight or spotlight. 
Innnediately after the 
light disappeared, the source ran to his house, about 25 yards away, and checked the 
time which was 103oz. After checking the time he went back to his original position 
and continued to watch the area the light came from, however, the light did not 
reappear. For location of the light see Incl #1. 
IV. 
WEATHER: 
The night was dark but with no overcast, stars were clearly visible. 
Big Diomede Island was plainly outlined against the sky. Wind was from the north 
and estimated at about 5 MPH. 
COMMENTS OF THE PREPARING OFFICER: 
The location of the light is considered somewhat unusual because people 
have never been observed on that part of Big Diomede Island and because of the 
difficulty in reaching the location, particularly at night. If persons on Bi g 
Diomede wanted to signal to the Alaskan ma.inland, it could best be done from the 
southern tip of the island.

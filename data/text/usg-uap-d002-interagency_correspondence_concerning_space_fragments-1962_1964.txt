RE: 
Queries on Fragment Return from NASA Spacecraft or Space Vehicles 
A much abbreviated guideline for RrQ, has been provided by Mr. Scheer 
to various NASA officials who might be queried on this subject. 
The 
following more extensive responses are provided as background information 
to be used by the Administrator, Deputy Administrator, or Associate 
Administrator of NASA, in their judgment, in event of queries on the 
• 
return of space vehicle fragments to the earth. It is suggested that 
the minimum inf_ormation be used to satisfy a given query. 
(A) 
FOR USE PRIOR TO AND AFTER A PARTICUIAR IAUNCH BUT BEFORE ANY KNOWN 
IMPACT OF RETURNING FRAGMENTS: 
l. Do fragments from (satellite X) pose a threat to life or 
property? 
The probability that fragments from (satellite X) may cause 
damage or injury is remote. 
The probability of injury from 
natural meteorites is far greater. 
2. a. What is the probability of impact? 
There are many factors (e.g., time in orbit, atmospheric 
conditions, how the vehicle breaks up, dispersal pattern, etc.) 
involved and the calculations are extremely complex. 
We have 
assured ourselves, however, that the hazard is far less than 
many hazards encountered in everyday living. 
3. 
Has NASA taken steps to lessen the fragment problem? 
Yes. 
NASA is actively engaged in minimizing it. For example, 
on the SA-5 satellite, NASA used 13,000 lbs. of harmless sand 
as ballast and roughed up the nosecone so that it would burn 
on reentry. 
On the GT-1 spacecraft, NASA bored large holes in 
the heat shield to assist burning during reentry. In addition 
to specific steps such as these, studies are underway to assist 
NASA in further coping with the overall reentry problem.

FOR INTERAGENCY HANDLING OF SPACE VEHICLE FRAGMENTS

1, The National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) is
designated as the agency responsible for receiving, _[ analyzing] and
identifying [any] fragments, or suspected fragments of space vehicles
of all types, including the contents thereof, which fall upon the territory
of the United States or which otherwise come into its possession, except

that the Department of Defense (DOD) is solely responsible for receiving
and identifying fragments from vehicles launched by the DOD,

{[2. NASA will turn over to the Department of Defense (DOD) as
expeditiously as possible fragments which are readily identifiable as coming
from space vehicles launched by the DOD. ]

2. In the event NASA should come into possession of space fragments
under the sole and exclusive cespensioi of the DOD, NASA will deliver
such fragments as expeditiously as possible to the DOD.

3. To insure prompt analysis for intelligence purposes, NASA and other
U. S. Government agencies will [ give] deliver tothe DOD[and other —
responsible agencies access] as expeditously as possible and on a priority
basis fragments (including whole vehicles) which are readily identifiable or
suspect as coming from | space] vehicles launched from foreign countries.
Such foreign fragments which come initially into possession of the DOD -
will be retained by DOD for analysis. Such analysis of foreign fragments
will be completed expeditiously, and thereafter such fragments will [remain]

be placed in the custody of NASA, pending final arrangements for their return
to the launching country or for other appropriate disposition,

4. All other fragments will be retained by NASA pending agreement as
to disposition,

5. NASA may continue to use the Smithsonian Institution for collection
and analysis of fragments by contractual arrangements, and may also make
appropriate arrangements for assistance with private agencies.

SUBJECT: Guidelines for Interagency Handling of Space Vehicle
Fragments
The President has approved the following procedures:
1, The National Aeronautics and Space Administration is designated
as the agency responsible for receiving, analyzing, and identifying
any fragments, or suspected fragments of space vehicles of all types,
including the contents thereof, which fall upon the territory of the

United States or which otherwise come into its possession.

2. NASA will turn over to the Department of Defense as expeditiously
as possible fragments which are readily identifiable as coming from
space vehicles launched by the Department of Defense.

3. Fragments identified as coming from vehicles launched by foreign
countries will be retained by NASA pending final arrangements for
their return to the launching country, or other disposition.

4. All other fragments will be retained by NASA pending agreement as
to disposition.

5. The Department of Defense will notify NASA when deorbits

EXECUTIVE OFFICE OF THE PRESIDENT 
NATIONAL AERONAUTICS and SPACE COUNCIL 
WASHINGTON 
EXECUTIVE SECRETARY 
October 2, 1962 
MEMORANDUM FOR 
. Honorable McGeorge Bundy 
Special Assistant to the President 
for National Security Affairs 
Subject: Responsibility and Procedure Re Space Vehicle Fragments. 
It has been suggested that there is need for a decision as to what 
government agencies should have what responsibilities in regard 
to obtaining, analyzing, and identifying fragments or suspected 
fragments from space vehicles which fall on U.S. territory or 
which otherwise come into U.S. possession. 
It has likewise been suggested that the National Aeronautics and 
Space Council is the appropriate agency to work out a coordinated 
position on this matter. There are legal as well as operational 
problems involved, and hence it would be helpful if this combina­
tion of issues were kept in mind in identifying a person or persons 
from your agency to participate in a meeting on this matter. 
To the extent that agreements or recommendations have already 
been made on this problem, information re garding them would 
contribute to such deliberations. 
I would appreciate your having my office (Code 113 - Extension 2203) 
advised as to who would represent your agency. Such individual or 
individuals will then be contacted for the purpose of determining an 
appropriate time for a meeting. 
E. C. Welsh

PUBLIC STATEMENT 
ON HANDLING OF SPACE VEHICLE FRAGMENTS 
The National Aeronautics and Space Administration is designated 
as the Government agency responsible for receiving, analyzing, and 
identifying any fragments or suspected fragments of space vehicles of 
all types, including the contents thereof, which fall upon the territory 
of the United States or which otherwise come into its possession. 
Fragments identified as coming from vehicles launched by foreign 
countries will be retained by NASA pending final arrangements for their 
return to the launching country or for other appropriate disposition. 
In performing these functions, NASA will coordinate fully with 
other Government agencies.

Draft Statement for Consideration at Meeting on October 17, 1962 
2:30 p. m. -- Room 218 E. O. B. 
The National Aeronautics and Space Administration has 
been designated as the Government agency responsible for obtaining, 
analyzing, and identifying any fragments or suspected fragments from 
space vehicles of all types which fall upon the territory of the United 
States or which are turned over to the United States by foreign coun­
tries. The only exception to this responsibility is that fragments 
which are readily identifiable as coming from space vehicles launched 
by the Department 0£ Defense will be turned over to the DOD as 
expeditiously as possible. NASA will be responsible for the disposition 
of all fragments which do not come from DOD vehicles. Fragments 
identified as coming from vehicles launched by foreign countries will 
be retained by NASA pending final arrangements for their return to the 
launching country. 
In performing these functions, NASA will coordinate fully 
with other interested government and private agencies.

Utah Ranch Report Abstract 
The "Utah Ranch" investigation is an ongoing project which to date has incorporated 
several deployments of investigators, scientists and engineers. The primary focus of 
the Utah ranch investigations are: 
• 
To comprehensively interview a large number of people who have lived in the 
area surrounding the Utah Ranch in order to delineate the scope and variety of 
eyewitness reports in the area. The purpose is to establish commonalities and 
differences with the ongoing reports of unusual activity on the ranch property 
itself. 
• 
To use portable sensor equipment to measure baseline magnetic field 
parameters, EM spectrum data, ionizing radiation and chemical elemental 
analysis of soil on the Utah Ranch property and surrounding areas. It goes 
without saying a primary purpose is to obtain definitive photographic, digital, 
sound and chemical evidence of purported anomalies. 
• 
To use plants, including transgenic plants, as biosensors of multiple radiation and 
magnetic field deviations from the norm on the ranch property. These plant 
experiments have been tailored as user friendly reporting systems for 
electromagnetic energy detection based on extensive research of the peer 
reviewed literature. 
• 
To initiate a variety of games, puzzles, and other strategies in order to establish 
communication with the source/origin of reported anomalies on the ranch 
property. 
Witnesses have reported unidentified balls of light at close range, daytime sightings of 
"dog-like" creatures running on two legs, unnatural animalistic howling/screaming, "little 
people", prehistoric-like birds and a maggot which measured about 8-feet long and 2 to 
3-feet in diameter. 
To date, May 7, 2010, over twenty-five (25) people have been contacted and 
interviewed in person, predominantly at their homes. This group consists of all life-long 
residents of the area within a mile or two of the ranch property, four (4) local police 
officers, eleven (11) Native Americas, including a Shaman, the director of the local Fish 
and Wildlife office, twelve (12) ranchers, six (6) business owners and approximately 
fifteen (15) members of the Mormon Church. All of the people interviewed have had

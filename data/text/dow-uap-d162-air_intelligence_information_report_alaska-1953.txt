I. This report contains information on a light seen by an observer at Gambell,
St. Lawrence Island, Alaska (63°50'N - 171°36'W), 011110Z December 1953. Light was
observed on a true heading of 283° from Gambell,

II, SOURCE:
Name = CLIFFORD IKNOKINOK
Age = 26
Race = Eskimo

Education = 8th Grade Completed

Militery Service - Member of Gambell Unit, Alaska National Guard for three
Tene ee, (3) years, 9 months, promoted to Sct 1 Jan 1953, Source
presently Squad Leader with Gambell Guard Unit.

Civilian Employment - Employed by CAA at Gambell from July 1942 thru April
1949, Present occupation -- hunter,

WILLIAM W, CALDWELL and RODERICK M. MOORE, members of Flight ‘At, 5004th AISS,
and stationed at Gambell, have known source for the past eight months. They have
found source to be trustworthy and honest and they have confidence in his judgement,

III, REPORT ON OBSERVATION:

Source states that approximately 1110Z, 1 December 53 he saw a light on the
horizon West of Gambell. Source was standing in front of the Gambell School (elev.
approx, 25 ft. above sea level) when he observed the light. Source said he thought
the light was about 10 inches in diameter. Later he said thet a circular object
5% inches in diameter, held at arms length, would cover the light. Source stated
that the light did not move and determined this by sighting along the flag pole at
the school, Source saw no reflection or beam of light and the color of the light
was similar to a flashlight. Source stated the light was not bright, nor was it
very dim. At 1115Z the light disappeared as though someone had turned it off.

Source observed the light without the aid of binoculars. Source would not
make an estimate as to distance of light from Gambell.

IV, WEATHER:

Wind = NE at approx. 20 MPH
Visibility - Approx. 20 miles
Ceiling = Approx. 2000 ft.

The night was very dark. There was slush ice West of Gambell but no heavy
ice was in the area. The ocean was calm,

V.. COMMENTS OF PREPARTNG OFFICER:

WILLIAM W. CALDWELL, member of Flight A, 5004th AISS, at Gambell, used a Len-
satic Compass to determine the true bearing of the light observed. He also made a

check of the village to see if other natives had observed the light but none had
seen the light.

Source is apparently in error when he says a disk 54 inches in diameter would
cover the light. A light measured in this manner would appear much larger than his
first estimate of the light size,

Alaska on @ bearing of 283° true, thus removing the possibility of a land based
light. From the information available no conclusion can be reached.as to the origin
